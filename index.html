Learn Data-Centric AI with Phoebe

Most effort in machine learning goes into the model while the dataset is treated as given. Data-centric work reverses that: freeze the model, freeze the test set, and make every improvement an edit to the training rows or their labels, measured like a code change. On 1,797 real handwritten digits with 133 declared wrong labels, cleaning them with confident learning takes one frozen model from 91.4 to 95.8 percent, while 497 more rows from the same labellers take it to 90.8. Six sessions for practitioners: label consistency, finding wrong labels, a bench where every edit is computed in your browser, slicing, augmentation against collection, and the spec that makes the edits survive the next model.

The six sessions 🎯 · from "the data is the problem" to a spec someone can audit

The running data is the UCI Optical Recognition of Handwritten Digits set: 1,797 real 8 by 8 images, one frozen k-nearest-neighbour model, a clean test set nobody edits, and a declared label-noise plant. Real cases join it from Andrew Ng's 2021 talk, the Data-Centric AI Competition, MIT's data-centric AI course, and the label errors found in ten famous benchmark test sets.

What is honest here 🧾

The images and every number the bench prints are real computations. The noise is planted on purpose and declared in full, because that is the only way to count what a detector missed. Where a figure comes from a paper or a talk, the page says which; where it comes only from secondary write-ups, the page says that instead.
